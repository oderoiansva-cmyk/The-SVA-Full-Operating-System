The Identity

What SVA Is

SVA is a strategic value architecture firm. It is not a consulting firm. It is not a data company. It is not a report shop. It is an accountability company — the layer of judgment, synthesis, and implementation protection that sits above the data cloud and below the boardroom.

The Positioning — One Sentence SVA combines the diagnostic rigor of MBB with the speed and accessibility of a solo consultant, protected by a 5-layer implementation protocol that removes client risk — and wins because the incumbents are structurally incapable of following.

Part 06

The Client Journey

Stage 1 — Marketing · The Hypothesis

Hypothesis

The SIS is built from public data, industry intelligence, competitor analysis, and observable signals. The 4-email sequence is sent according to the updated schedule. The briefing page is live.

Output: SIS + 4 emails + Strategic Briefing. The tension is a hypothesis.
Stage 2 — Sales · The Conversion

Conversion

The prospect opens 3 consecutive emails. You send Follow-Up 1 (Protocol 01). If they reply, you book the diagnostic. If they don't, you send Follow-Up 2 (Protocol 02) 2–3 weeks later. If they call, you use Protocol 09A.

Output: Diagnostic booked. MSA + SOW signed. 50% upfront paid. The SOW names the tension as preliminary — to be confirmed or revised.
Stage 3 — Contracting · The Legal Instruments

Legal Instruments

MSA (Protocol 07) and SOW (Protocol 08) are generated from the inputs. If registered, the business entity signs. If not, the interim protocol applies.

Output: Signed MSA + SOW. Payment received.
Stage 4 — Diagnostic · The Validation

Validation

Two weeks. Data request. 90-minute CEO session (Protocol 03). CFO session. The Revised SIS (Protocol 04A) is produced. The Diagnostic Report (Protocol 04) is delivered.

Output: Revised SIS (Gate 1). Diagnostic Report. Full engagement proposal. The tension is confirmed, refined, or replaced. The delta is the value.
Stage 5 — Full Engagement · The Protection

Protection

12 weeks. Four phases. Four gates. The 5-Layer Protection Protocol is operationalized (Protocol 05).

Output: Strategy, roadmap, IRA, pre-mortem, final report. Full payment. The client owns the execution. SVA owns the advice.
Stage 6 — Referral · The Compound

Compound

Within 7 days of engagement close, the referral request is sent (Protocol 06). Specific names. Easy introductions. Genuine release.

Output: 1–3 referrals per satisfied client. The referral loop is the multiplier.

Part 08

The Protection Protocol — 5 Layers

1
MSA Clause 4 · SOW Scope section

Contractual Protection

“The Consultant recommends. The Client executes. The Consultant is not liable for implementation.”

Why it matters: It draws the line before work begins. The client cannot claim you were responsible for execution.

2
SOW Section 4 — Gates 1–4

Structural Protection (Sign-Off Gates)

Each gate is a written sign-off. Payment is triggered by gate, not by time.

Why it matters: Once a gate is signed, you cannot be held accountable for failure at a later gate.

3
SOW Section 9 · Delivered at Gate 3 (draft) and Gate 4 (final)

Operational Protection (IRA)

The Implementation Readiness Assessment scores the client's readiness across six dimensions. Red flags are documented.

Why it matters: If the client proceeds despite red flags, the record shows you warned them.

4
SOW Section 3 — Phase 4 deliverable

Monitoring Protection (Tracker)

The Implementation Tracker tracks milestones, budget burn, risk triggers, and dependencies. The client's Project Lead updates it. The Steering Committee reviews it.

Why it matters: It creates a paper trail. If the client deviates, it is visible and documented.

5
SOW Section 8 · Pre-Mortem at Gate 4

Governance Protection (Steering Committee + Pre-Mortem)

The Joint Steering Committee is constituted, chaired by the client CEO, with formal minutes. The Pre-Mortem Documentation identifies risks and states: “Implementation recommendations are at Client's discretion.”

Why it matters: Decisions and acknowledgments are recorded. The client cannot later deny what was agreed. The Pre-Mortem is the insurance policy.

The Trigger Event Exception

A Tier 3 prospect can warm up. Watch for:

  • Leadership change — new CEO, CFO.
  • Funding event — new investment, exit.
  • Industry disruption — regulatory change, competitor move.
  • Public distress signal.
Rule If a trigger event occurs, re-engage regardless of tier.

Part 12

The Operating Principles

The Ten Rules of SVA

  1. Evidence beats opinion. Every claim traces to a number.
  2. Speed is a feature. Two-week diagnostic. Not six-week proposal.
  3. Protection is the product. The 5-Layer Protocol is why clients choose SVA.
  4. Specificity is the moat. If it could be sent to another client, it is not finished.
  5. Release is the close. The autonomy email is what makes the decision real.
  6. Tiering is the discipline. Not everyone gets re-engaged. Focus.
  7. The referral loop compounds. Ask once, specifically, easily.
  8. The pipeline is the leading indicator. Protect it above all else.
  9. Hire when delivery exceeds 60% of your time. The associate is not optional at scale.
  10. Decline incompatible clients. A bad fit is a future dispute.

The Quality Check — Every Output

Before anything leaves the desk:

  • Could this be sent to another client with only the name changed? → Rewrite.
  • Does it contain a specific number or fact? → If no, rewrite.
  • Does it continue the arc? → If it restarts, rewrite.
  • Does it respect the Shield? → If not, rewrite.
  • Is there any sentence that could be deleted without loss? → Delete it.
Part 13

The Annual Review

The Year-End Reset — December

StepAction
1Complete outstanding engagements.
2Run the full annual review: what worked, what didn't, what to change.
3Re-tier all accounts for the new year.
4Prepare Q1 strategy.
5Rest.

The Questions to Ask

  • What was the revenue? Actual vs. target.
  • What was the pipeline health at year-end? Tier 1 count. Tier 2 count.
  • What was the conversion rate? Prospect → meeting → diagnostic → engagement.
  • What was the churn? Client retention.
  • What was the referral rate? Referrals per client.
  • What was the associate capacity? Did the associate hire work?
  • What was the biggest risk that materialized? What did it teach?
  • What is the one thing to change for next year?
The Output A one-page Annual Review that becomes the INPUT for the Q1 Strategic Reset in January.
